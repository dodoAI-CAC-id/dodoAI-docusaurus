# Wireframe – Definition Guide

- Use this guide to clarify expectations, standards, and best practices for wireframe creation in your project.
- A wireframe is a visual, low-fidelity framework of an application’s interface, focusing on layout, element placement, and user experience flow.
- Do not reuse demo/sample designs. Always create wireframes that are specific to your project’s requirements and stakeholders.

---

## 1. Purpose and Value

- **Establish Structure:**  
  Wireframes provide the foundational structure for an application or web page, informing detailed design and development decisions.

- **Stakeholder Feedback:**  
  Wireframes are essential for capturing and iteratively refining stakeholder input early in the design phase, helping align vision and expectations.

- **Identify Usability Issues:**  
  Early visualization of layout and navigation allows the team to spot and resolve potential UX and workflow challenges before development.

- **Team Alignment:**  
  Wireframes act as a shared reference, supporting unified understanding across design, development, product ownership, and client teams.

---

## 2. Approaches for Wireframe Creation

### From Scratch

- Build interfaces starting from blank canvas, guided by UX best practices and references.
- Follow established methodologies and step-by-step guides (e.g., [Uizard Wireframing Guide](https://uizard.io/blog/website-wireframing-a-step-by-step-guide/)) to ensure thoughtful structure and navigation.
- Ideal for projects with unique flows, branding, or requirements.

### Using a Design System

- Leverage pre-built components, layouts, and style rules from the organization’s Design System.
- Ensures rapid construction, design consistency, and brand compliance.
- Particularly effective for products expanding on existing interface standards.

---

## 3. Figma as the Standard Wireframing Tool

- **Collaboration:**  
  Figma enables real-time, cloud-based collaboration between designers, developers, and stakeholders.

- **Accessibility and Permissions:**  
  All project members should have access to the Figma workspace. Contact your manager or design lead if you need permissions.

- **Templates and Components:**  
  Reuse available wireframe templates or create new boards within Figma as needed. Utilize the Design System’s components when available for consistency.

- **Version Control:**  
  Track wireframe changes and comments within Figma to maintain a clear evolution path.

---

## 4. Wireframe Output Guidelines

- Focus on layout, navigation, and content hierarchy over visual detail or finalized copy.
- Label functional zones (header, navigation, content, sidebar, footer, dialogs, etc.) clearly.
- Use placeholders and annotations to indicate important interactions, flows, or intended dynamic behaviors.
- Regularly review and iterate wireframes based on team and stakeholder feedback.
- All final wireframes should be archived in the project’s Figma workspace for reference throughout the lifecycle.

---

## 5. Tips for Effective Wireframing

- Engage stakeholders/involved team members early and often in wireframe reviews.
- Prioritize clarity and usability in all layouts.
- Resist premature styling—keep wireframes free of final colors, detailed images, or branding unless driven by an existing Design System.
- Document open issues or questions directly inside the wireframe with comment markers.

---

**How to Use This Guide:**  
Apply this standard to every project wireframe session, ensuring clear process and output, maximum re-use of organizational assets, and ongoing collaborative feedback for high-quality, user-centered design.
